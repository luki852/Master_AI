 Theorem (Noether)
 Every continuous symmetry of a physical system corresponds
 to a conservation law.

Examples:

 ■ Time symmetry → energy conservation
 ■ Spatial symmetry → momentum conservation
 ■ Rotational symmetry → angular momentum

Partial Differential Equations
A PDE relates a function and its derivatives.


                     ∆(x, u, ux , uxx , . . . ) = 0

where
 ■ x : spatial+temporal coordinates
 ■ u(x) : unknown solution
 ■ ux , uxx , · · · : collection of derivatives of respective orders
Example: Heat Equation


                           αuxx − ut = 0

This equation describes how heat diffuses over time.

Symmetries of PDEs



Also here (see lecture): symmetry transformation mapping
one solution to another:


                   (x, u(x)) → (gx, gu(x))

Important: gu(x) again satisfies PDE.




                                                       6/11
Lie Point Symmetries


 ■ Specific class of symmetries acting on PDE solutions
 ■ Form Lie Groups
 ■ Each symmetry can be written as g(ε) for ε > 0
 ■ Multiple transformations can be combined:

                    g = g1 (ε1 )g2 (ε2 ) . . . gr (εr )

 ■ Allows generating a large family of new solutions
 ■ Precise mathematical theory/algorithmic recipe
 ■ Known already for a large class of PDEs

Example: Korteweg–de Vries Equation

                                                           ut + uux + uxxx = 0

It describes solitons in shallow water waves.
Important symmetries:
 ■ Time translation: g1 (ε)u = u(x, t + ε)
 ■ Space translation: g2 (ε)u = u(x + ε, t)
 ■ Galilean boost: g3 (ε)u = u(x − εt, t) + ε
 ■ Scaling transformation: g4 (ε)u = e−2ε u(eε x, e3ε t)

Symmetry-Based Data Augmentation

 1. Find all one-parameter symmetries g1 , · · · , gr
 2. Sample transformation parameters εi ∼ pi
 3. Compute g = g1 (ε1 )g2 (ε2 ) . . . gr (εr )
 4. Transform samples (x, u(x)) → (gx, gu(x))
 5. Reinterpolate to grid (in case domain changes)
 6. Add to dataset
